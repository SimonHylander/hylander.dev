## Problem

Buying second-hand from a stranger online means one side has to trust the other. The buyer pays first and hopes the item arrives, or the seller ships first and hopes to get paid. In 2020, 449,000 Swedes were exposed to sales fraud, according to the Swedish National Council for Crime Prevention (BRÅ).

Treddy's CEO started the company after paying for a used camera that never arrived. The goal was a service that sits between buyer and seller on any ad site, such as Facebook Marketplace, and removes the need for trust.

## My role

I led the technical architecture and development from September 2021 to April 2024, as a consultant through Skoglit. I built the consumer product that launched on treddy.se in early 2022, and from December 2022 the Treddy Developer Platform that let marketplaces build Treddy into their own sites.

## The product

Treddy is not a marketplace. The seller lists the item wherever they like, and Treddy handles the deal: identity, payment, shipping and support.

<Figure src={home} alt="The treddy.se home page: 'Buy, sell and ship second-hand. On Facebook and every other marketplace', with buttons to create or request a payment link." caption="The treddy.se home page in November 2022. Sellers create a payment link, buyers can request one." />

A trade runs in five steps:

1. **The seller creates a payment link.** They describe the item, choose shipping and get a link to share in the ad or with the buyer.
2. **The buyer pays.** Treddy holds the money until the buyer has the item.
3. **The seller ships.** Shipping is booked with DB Schenker automatically, and the seller gets the label by email.
4. **The buyer collects and inspects.** The buyer picks up the parcel at a Schenker agent and has 24 hours to inspect and approve it.
5. **The seller gets paid.** Treddy pays out once the buyer approves, or automatically after the 24 hours.

<Figure src={steps} alt="The five steps of a Treddy trade on a vertical timeline, from creating a payment link to the seller getting paid." caption="The five steps, as the home page explained them." />

Users sign in with BankID, so both sides of a trade are verified. Selling is free. The buyer pays shipping plus 2.5% of the price. The site was built to WCAG 2.1 AA.

## Decisions

### A payment link instead of a marketplace

A link works on every ad site without any integration on their side, so Treddy could launch without convincing a marketplace first. The seller creates it once, and anyone with the link can buy, the same way as in a web shop.

### Hold the money until the buyer approves

The buyer's money is locked when they pay and released only when the item is approved. The deal has a deadline at each step: the seller ships within 7 days, the buyer collects within 14 days and inspects within 24 hours. If the seller misses a deadline the buyer is refunded, an uncollected parcel goes back to the seller, and a buyer who does nothing approves the item. No deal stays open.

### Build every provider into one platform

Each part of a trade depends on an outside provider. I connected them behind one API layer that the consumer product and partners both use.

### Open the platform to marketplaces

The payment link brought Treddy to users. The Developer Platform brought it to marketplaces and rental sites, which could offer safe payment and shipping on their own listings. Partners got BankID verification and signing, locked payments, Schenker shipping labels and delivery tracking through one API, while Treddy handled customer support for the deal. I worked on the developer docs, covering authorization, the API reference, webhook events and webhook signatures, and helped onboard partners.
